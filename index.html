Cycling China: Practical Community Guide

Crowdsourced tips from the Cycling China WhatsApp group (active discussions mainly Jan–Sep 2026; group created Feb 2025). Experiences vary by city, officer, and season. Rules change—verify locally before you rely on any tip. Companion resources often cited in-chat: the group AI Digest Google Doc and the free Rolling Around cycle-touring app (community waypoints).

Visas & visa extensions

Visa-free vs sticker (L) visa

  • Many nationalities can enter visa-free for 30 days, with no official limit on number of entries or total days in a year (as long as each stay fits the purpose of tourism / transit—not work/study). Multiple embassies’ Q&As were shared in-chat supporting this.
  • Visa-free can be extended once for up to ~30 more days—but only in certain cities, and each PSB Exit-Entry office interprets the rules differently.
  • A proper L (tourism) sticker visa (often 60–90 days) is extendable in most prefecture-level cities. Group members repeatedly advise: if you want flexibility, get a 90-day visa before entry (e.g. Hanoi, Bangkok, Luang Prabang, Europe) rather than relying only on visa-free + one extension.
  • You cannot apply for a new Chinese visa while inside mainland China—only extend, or exit and re-enter (visa run).
  • Overstay: reported fine around ¥500/day; also risks future visa trouble.

Visa-free extension — cities that often refuse

  • Beijing, Qingdao, Nanning, Guilin — multiple denials.
  • Xi’an — mixed / “50-50”; one paper-visa extension took two weeks (not recommended for timing).
  • Yushu — once ran out of stickers; travelers sent to Xining.
  • North generally harder; southwest / west easier. Always have Plan B (next city, or visa run to HK / Vietnam / etc.).

Timing & paperwork quirks

  • Apply in the last week of your stay for visa-free (some offices earlier refuse).
  • While waiting, some offices say you must stay in that city / hostel; applying before expiry keeps you legal even if the sticker arrives after the original date ends.
  • Paper L visas: max extension typically cannot exceed original duration (e.g. 90-day visa → up to 30 days per extension; some offices allow multiple sequential extensions—Lijiang/Leshan/Xining more flexible than Chengdu/Kangding).
  • Hong Kong visa run: HSR from Guilin / Shenzhen is a common reset for multi-entry / visa-free eligible passports. For single-entry sticker visas, HK run does not refresh the same sticker.

Getting a visa abroad (examples from chat)

  • Luang Prabang: L-tourism 90 days reported; ~USD 26/person; passport back after ~4 business days.
  • Hanoi: works but can be slow; prepare docs early; mixed outcomes if itinerary/hotels incomplete.
  • Bangkok: online form + visa centre; ~€100 and ~3 weeks process for one report.
  • Invitation letter or flight + hotel bookings often requested; some SE Asia posts became looser (fewer hotels). Cancel bookings after issuance if entering by land/camping—common practice discussed.

Restricted / military / no-hotel counties

Foreigners face fully closed counties and conditionally restricted ones (Aliens’ Travel Permit / guided travel on paper). Enforcement is uneven: some cyclists pass unnoticed; others are stopped, held hours, or escorted out—usually politely.

Hunan corridor (Zhangjiajie ↔ Guilin) — concrete anecdote

Counties repeatedly named as no-hotel / restricted for foreigners:

  • Tongdao (often spelled Tongdoa in chat)
  • Jingzhou
  • Huitong
  • Hongjiang
  • Possibly Huaihua area

Detention anecdote: A cyclist was held ~8 hours in Huitong after a local selfie led to a police call; driven to Tongdao at ~8pm and told to cycle ~50 km south out of the county without talking to anyone. Not violent—“weird but chill,” but time-consuming. Another party took a slow train from Tongdao station (~40 km from town) to Huaihua next day without issues. Some later riders passed Jingzhou/Tongdao with police/military sightings and no stop—rules or luck may vary; overnight stays are the clearest red flag.

Sichuan / Leshan area

  • Jinkouhe District (金口河区) — treated as off-limits; G245 closure + police: Jinkou county described as entirely off-limits; cyclists escorted toward Wushu instead of canyon.
  • Restricted county between Ebian and Ganluo — train hop for a few km used by cyclists.
  • Seda (色达) listed in AI-compiled “totally forbidden” lists shared in-chat.

Qinghai / Gansu plateau

  • Qilian and Menyuan on G227: common advice—pass through by day, do not sleep overnight. Roadworks can force long detours (~140 km); some skip the risk.
  • Huzhu (near Xining): police stop reported—treat as sensitive.
  • Guide county: camping refused; escorted to hotel (hotel stay still possible in that case).
  • Cyclist stopped near Bazhaxiang (Xining→Wuwei), footage deleted, driven toward Gansu.
  • North side of Qinghai Lake: police escort out reported (courteous).

Xinjiang

  • Toward Aksu / Kashgar: alternative roads often blocked; police may insist on highway (wide shoulder; camping sometimes allowed). Hejing and others cited as off-limits for detours.
  • Duku Highway / grassland near Yining: friendly but persistent police escort / hotel placement reported.
  • South of Kashgar toward Tashkurgan: get a border permit in Kashgar.
  • Kulma (Qolma) Pass (Tajikistan): border→Kashgar not “restricted” in the same sense, but vehicle required—hitchhike/minibus; cycling the corridor not practical as described.
  • Accidental entry into a restricted military zone: hotel Trip.com “no foreigners” pattern is a clue; police interest in satellite messengers (InReach) reported.

Tibet Autonomous Region (TAR)

  • Independent cycling in TAR generally needs Tibet Entry Permit + agency/guide; not free-roam like Tibetan areas of Sichuan/Yunnan/Qinghai/Gansu.
  • Agency example shared: Tibet Vista / tibettravel.org (Lhasa→Gyirong/Nepal).
  • Distinguish TAR from “Tibetan cultural plateau” outside TAR (highly recommended by many cyclists; no Tibet Entry Permit).

Practical survival rules

  • Check Trip.com / hotel “foreigners not allowed”—clustered refusals often mean a closed county.
  • Use Amap/Gaode cycling mode to avoid expressways; Rolling Around waypoints for trouble spots.
  • Worst typical outcome: polite escort out or forced train/truck—not fines for most cyclists’ stories (fines 500–5000 RMB mentioned for unauthorized entry in compiled rules).
  • Negotiate direction of escort (continue your route, not backtrack).

Trains, buses & flying with bikes

Onboard the train (same train as you)

  • Usually free if accepted as luggage.
  • Typical pack: remove front wheel; wrap frame in plastic bags / cardboard / bubble wrap. Some stations accept minimal wrap; others insist on fuller coverage or a real box.
  • Hard sleeper: reported OK. HSR: large luggage space at end of car often fits bike + panniers. Older K trains: crowded, smoky, little space—avoid if possible.
  • Strictness varies wildly: Tongren and some stations demanded cardboard; Urumqi station reportedly needs a large bag (~¥20 at CRE under station) or full plastic wrap—bags+cardboard alone not enough; Nanning East had no wrapping materials.
  • Bike + bags go through X-ray—knives, gas, some fluids confiscated.
  • Reassemble on the platform on arrival—reported fine.

China Railway Express (CRE / 中铁快运)

  • Rail cargo between stations; bike travels separately, arrives ~1–few days later (sometimes before you).
  • Find office via Amap search “China Railway Express” / 中铁快运 (not always at every station).
  • Pricing examples: ~¥290 for 2 bikes ~90 kg Ürümqi→Lanzhou with ~21 days storage; Chengdu→Shenzhen ~¥250; Beijing→Kashgar ~€37 bike + ~€15 panniers; another report ~¥288/bike + ¥154 bags Beijing area.
  • Storage: Beijing CRE: fee formula shared as ¥3 × (days − 3) × items; 32 days OK. Other reports: ~¥3/day, multi-month OK if arranged; ask locally.
  • No train ticket always required to send; receiver can differ from sender.
  • CNY 2026: official sources said CRE operating; local offices still sometimes closed (e.g. Xishuangbanna)—call ahead.
  • Refusals: Honghe (near Vietnam) refused bikes once (staff still helped store/pack for passenger train). Qingdao North would only send toward Kunming not Dali. Some offices refuse knives, gas, chain oil, non-CCC electronics.
  • Alternative courier: SF Express (顺丰)—e.g. Lijiang→Beijing Airport ~€25/20 kg per box; Chinese phone number often needed.

Flying with a bike

  • Get cardboard from Giant / Merida / Trek / Decathlon-type shops (Kunming Trek near Decathlon has boxes outside; Giant Lijiang boxed for ¥80 each).
  • Airlines vary on weight (often 23 kg strictness—e.g. China Eastern / some Japan routes); dimensions matter.
  • Hard case vs cardboard: both used; shops help pack.

Ferries & land borders (bike notes)

  • Incheon → Yantai / Weihai / Dandong: bike onboard; Yantai report no extra charge. Qingdao–Incheon: ¥250/bike surcharge reported (newer rule).
  • Macau–Hong Kong ferry: bike possible HKD/MOP 65 with front wheel off (Cotai Water Jet luggage tariffs).
  • Takeshiken (Mongolia border) → Ürümqi: shared taxi ~¥250/person (Oct 2025) with packed bike, no bike surcharge; buses Ürümqi–Qinghe may charge full ticket for bike into driver’s pocket.
  • Kyrgyzstan land border: no-man’s-land minibus ~¥20; panniers off, bike usually intact.
  • Boten (Laos): described as easy; China-side road good near border.
  • Border trains often won’t take bikes onboard—ship CRE, cross, continue.

Power banks, knives & prohibited items

Power banks & CCC

  • Domestic China flights (rule from ~Jun/Jul 2025): Chinese-made power banks without CCC mark often confiscated in carry-on. Non-China-made without CCC sometimes OK. Ref: LoyaltyLobby summary.
  • Trains: capacity talk centers on ~20,000 mAh and CCC; enforcement inconsistent—some never checked; some confiscate. Prefer CCC-marked units; protect printed Wh/mAh labels (rubbed-off specs caused confiscation).
  • Land borders: usually OK for power banks/knives if cycling (planes/trains are the problem).
  • CRE: often refuses power banks / gas / lighters / chain lube; take those with you on passenger train or mail separately (~¥25, 6–7 days Kashgar→Xining example after confiscation).
  • Prefer Wh rating: airline logic often <100 Wh; China adds CCC layer.

Knives

  • Train security: blade ≤ ~6 cm (60 mm) typically allowed; longer blades measured and may be taken.
  • Confiscation ≠ criminal usually; stations may courier the knife to your destination address (need WeChat + address).
  • CRE/SF often refuse knives; people hide in tool kits / frame bags with mixed success—not guaranteed.
  • Land entry by bike: knives often OK.

Other

  • No gas canisters, lighters, flammables on trains/CRE.
  • Fresh produce sometimes seized at borders.

Bike shops, repairs & parts by city

  • General: Most cities have Giant / Merida / XDS; fine for chains, pads, basic service. Specific touring parts → Taobao; second-hand → Xianyu (闲鱼). Boskey mentioned as good Chinese touring brand.
  • 36-hole rims: hard to find in China (and Vietnam)—plan ahead.
  • Kunming: 胖轮胎自行车俱乐部 (大观店) / Pangluntai — 大观路260号, Amap — traveler-owned, repairs + storage (~1 month arranged). Hostel storage: i野·倾城国际青年旅舍 (大梅园巷店), ~¥30/day/item, Amap.
  • Chengdu: well-regarded unnamed shop boxed bike, ordered van to airport bus, good Shimano stock (photo shared in chat—ask group / Rolling Around). Merida / XDS also used. Wheel builds requested often; competence varies.
  • Qingdao Giant: owner Ling—English, lent tools for freehub clean—highly praised.
  • Lijiang Giant (Ancient Town): Amap; boxing ¥80.
  • Kashgar: bike-shop cluster Amap area; more pro shop link. Basics OK; specialty parts order online.
  • Aksu: chain swap ~20 min, parts price only.
  • Songpan / Xining: mechanics exist; Xining better stocked at plateau edge.
  • Litang: limited—broken spokes hard to fix locally.
  • Jinghong (Xishuangbanna): decent shops in a mid-size city.
  • Fuel for stoves: Xinjiang petrol stations may refuse foreigners—try car mechanic workshops.

Routes & regions

Highly recommended (community)

  • Tibetan areas outside TAR: west Sichuan, north Yunnan, Gansu/Qinghai plateau—culture, altitude, scenery; freer than TAR. Example praise: Gansu/Sichuan Tibetan regions >3000 m, friendly locals, cheap beds.
  • Xishuangbanna → Shangri-La → Xiangcheng (乡城) and along Sichuan–Qinghai Tibetan fringe; high road to ~4300 m newly paved/quiet (local long-term resident tip).
  • Xinjiang–Gansu–Sichuan–Yunnan full traverse (Khorgas → Lao Cai) done solo by multiple people with camping.
  • Yangtze / karst near Pingxiang, Guilin area scenery.
  • Community route maps with green “liked” segments: zweiradlertouren ZAT FAQ route, Google maps overview, hackerbikepacker.

Zhangjiajie

  • Extremely touristy / commercial; unique mountains on a clear day.
  • Worth ~1 day for many; winter/low season much better (park ~3% capacity reported)—stay inside park near west gate for solitude at sunrise/sunset. Multi-day ticket ~$30 range mentioned; cable cars optional/expensive queues in peak.
  • Avoid dense haze/fog; rain/mist can look dramatic.
  • Route south to Guilin: avoid restricted counties listed above; swing east.

Guilin / Yangshuo

  • Generally preferred scenery vs Zhangjiajie crowds; quiet 民宿 ~9 km from Yangshuo center recommended in winter.

Avoid / caution

  • Heavy-truck narrow tunnel roads that are main Qinghai/Tibet connectors (look quiet on map, dangerous in reality)—Yunnan NW Sichuan/Gansu tip.
  • Expressways: usually banned for bikes; police may force hitch for short segments (e.g. Lake Sayram downhill).
  • Southern Yunnan: lots of rain; flooding/landslides north of Guilin (Tongdao area, Jun 2026 report).

Altitude / plateau logistics

  • Fuel for stoves: ask locals/motorbike users on plateau.
  • Tibetan language: Google Translate Tibetan dialects unreliable; WeChat translate for Mandarin-literate contacts.

Accommodation for cyclists

  • Hotels must accept foreigners by law except in restricted/sensitive areas. Trip.com “no foreigners” banners are often wrong—book anyway, then insist / call police to register.
  • Staff often don’t know the passport dropdown on the national registration site (身份证 → 护照/外籍). Offer to help; police usually come and complete registration.
  • Refusals happen anyway (Han-majority cities sometimes worse; minority areas sometimes easier). Worst cases: police eject at night even after check-in—have Plan B (next town, camp if safe).
  • Camping: widely practiced; city parks, barbecue gardens, roadside. Free or ¥40–50 informal sites. Discreet camping in parks OK-ish; remote Xinjiang camping often fine until police “security” escorts appear.
  • Warmshowers / Couchsurfing / hostel courtyards / bike shops for storage during visa runs.
  • Registering while camping: law says notify police within 24h—rarely enforced for short wild camps per long-stay cyclists; hotels risk fines if they fail to register you, not you.

Money & payments

  • Daily life is QR codes; cash still works but change can be awkward—carry small notes (~¥100 emergency stash).
  • Link international card to Alipay/WeChat; Mastercard “protect” may block repeated WeChat Pay—try Alipay or Nihao China; fix phone battery/app-switching settings if bank approval fails.
  • Cash exchange at Bank of China: bureaucratic (~1h), passport required.
  • Visa extension offices often cash or WeChat/Alipay only—no foreign plastic.

Customs, shipping & leaving with a bike

  • Leaving China by air: box at bike shop; watch weight and chain-oil in the box (opened at security).
  • Shipping bike domestically via CRE/SF is easy; exporting mid-trip less discussed—plan airline rules.
  • Giant store-to-store shipping mentioned as alternative to CRE.
  • Store bike at CRE or shop during visa run (Beijing CRE multi-week storage OK).

Safety, police & registration

  • Police interactions in restricted areas: generally friendly, slow, photo-of-passport heavy; delete photos if asked in military zones (Dong Van border area anecdote: 1h passport + photo scroll).
  • Hotels refusing without legal basis: calmly ask them to call police to register—often resolves.
  • Traffic: tunnels + trucks on “quiet” mountain connectors are the real danger called out in-chat.
  • Knives/power banks: see above—main hassle is stations/airports, not street crime stories.

Weather & seasonal notes

  • Winter Zhangjiajie / Guilin: fewer crowds, pleasant hiking; cold nights on plateau.
  • Southern Yunnan: expect rain; NW Sichuan/Gansu plateau often better weather windows.
  • Summer: flooding/landslides possible (e.g. Tongdao region Jun 2026).
  • Chinese New Year: CRE officially open but local offices may close; expect slower logistics; confirm in person.
  • Plateau nights cold even when days warm—cheap indoor beds plentiful on some Tibetan routes.

Other actionable tips

  • Highway cycling: rare exceptions (Xinjiang shoulder segments with police blessing); default = stay on G/S roads that Amap cycling allows.
  • Bus with bikes: drivers vary; full bike fare into pocket happens; cardboard/plastic wrap helps acceptance.
  • Hong Kong side trip: leave bike in Shenzhen if only visiting central HK (2–4h public transport + immigration).
  • Group AI Digest: Google Doc—search before asking repeats.
  • Strava / Komoot / Bike Map: elevation estimates unreliable; cross-check contours.
